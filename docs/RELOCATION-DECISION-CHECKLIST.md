# Relocation decision checklist

This checklist helps an agent or household turn a serious relocation question
into transparent scenarios and research. It is country-neutral: it records
facts, evidence, uncertainty, and choices without producing a single opaque
score or a recommendation.

Use it with a Wayfinder document. Keep private household information in local,
ignored data only; this public checklist must contain no real figures, names,
employers, addresses, account details, or private links.

## 1. Establish the decision frame

- State the decision to be made, the intended decision date, and the planning
  horizon.
- List the realistic destinations or stay-put alternative being compared.
- Record the current household baseline: household members, work arrangements,
  care responsibilities, housing situation, financial commitments, and what
  must remain available in the home country.
- Define non-negotiables separately from preferences. Examples include legal
  eligibility, dependent safety, minimum cash runway, ability to service an
  existing obligation, or access to required care.
- List the decisions that are linked but distinct: accepting a role, selecting
  a permit route, moving first, bringing dependents, changing jobs, renewing a
  lease, or returning.

## 2. Keep evidence types separate

Every item below should be classified before it affects a scenario.

| Type | What it contains | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official rule | Government or statutory eligibility, permit conditions, tax, health coverage, school eligibility, and regulated deadlines | Cite the responsible authority, capture the retrieval date, and mark conditions and exceptions. |
| Market estimate | Salary range, rent, utilities, moving services, insurance, flights, childcare availability, commute, or other changing market information | Record source type, location scope, date, range, assumptions, and confidence. Refresh before a binding choice. |
| User fact | Household income, savings, debt, family need, offer terms, preferences, or timing | Keep only in the private Wayfinder document. Mark whether confirmed, estimated, or pending. |
| Open question | A material unknown that cannot safely be assumed | Give it an owner, needed evidence, deadline, and the scenarios affected. |

Do not convert source quality, lifestyle preference, or uncertainty into a
numeric quality score. A scenario can show its assumptions and unresolved
questions directly.

## 3. Build explicit Wayfinder scenarios

Create one scenario for each meaningful branch, rather than hiding branches in
one blended projection. Typical branches include:

- stay in the current location;
- move with one income versus two incomes;
- solo-first move with dependents joining later;
- spouse employed promptly, delayed, or not employed during the trial period;
- employer-supported versus self-funded relocation;
- current role retained, local job switch, permit-linked role change, or a
  cross-country alternative;
- lower, expected, and higher cost or income assumptions; and
- a six-month trial followed by continuation or return.

For each scenario, record: its purpose; who moves and when; permit and work
assumptions; income sources; expense assumptions; one-time costs; savings and
emergency-fund treatment; research records; open questions; and the exact
event that would trigger a revisit, stop, or return. Use the same shared
household fields across options and option-specific fields only where the
scenario differs.

Turn each branch into either:

- a separate Wayfinder scenario when it changes cash flow, legal status,
  timing, household composition, or a decision gate; or
- a research item when evidence could resolve the branch before modelling it.

For example, uncertain dependent work authorization is a research item until
confirmed. If more than one legally possible outcome remains material, model
each outcome as a separate scenario with the rule and date attached.

## 4. Check income, work, and permits

- Capture compensation as gross pay, not only take-home pay. Separate taxes,
  mandatory deductions, automatic retirement or pension contributions, and
  employer-paid benefits from net cash.
- Model each household income independently, including start dates, currency,
  contract security, probation risk, variable compensation, and realistic
  employment gaps.
- Test one-income affordability before relying on a second income.
- Research spouse/dependent work rights, conditions, processing time, renewals,
  and responsibility for dependent status.
- Research whether a permit is employer-specific, whether a job switch is
  permitted, the transition process, and any consequences for dependents.
- Identify viable cross-country alternatives and their separate permit,
  tax, and household implications; do not assume a right in one jurisdiction
  transfers to another.
- Record employer benefits and limitations: relocation support, immigration
  support, insurance, pension match, leave, childcare support, temporary
  housing, tax assistance, and return or repayment clauses.
- Run an income-growth sensitivity: model at least a 5% and 10% change in each
  material income, separately from cost changes.

## 5. Check money in two time horizons

### Before and during the move

- Visa, permit, medical, translation, legal, and document costs.
- Tickets, shipping, baggage, temporary accommodation, and local setup.
- Rental deposits, advance rent, broker or tenant fees, furnishings, and
  utility/mobile/internet activation.
- Employment-gap costs, currency conversion, taxes due on transition, and
  contingency for processing delays or a failed move.
- Reimbursable employer support versus costs paid first by the household.

### Recurring household cash flow

- Gross-to-net tax and payroll treatment, including retirement contributions.
- Rent, utilities, mobile, internet, groceries, transport, and health costs.
- Childcare, education, family visits, and dependent-related costs.
- Office requirements, commute, neighbourhood fit, and any vehicle or remote
  work needs.
- Continuing home-country debts, insurance, remittances, property costs,
  investments, tax duties, and return travel.
- Planned savings and investments, kept distinct from consumption expenses.

Use dated ranges for market estimates. Reconcile every scenario so income,
expenses, planned investments, and cash remaining are clear; do not treat a
retirement contribution or investment as an ordinary expense.

## 6. Check lived feasibility, not only affordability

- Compare neighbourhood, commute, office expectations, school or childcare
  access, family visitor practicality, and daily support networks.
- Record weather, language, accessibility, safety considerations, lifestyle,
  and work-life-balance expectations as qualitative notes with evidence where
  possible.
- Distinguish a personal preference from a factual claim. State whose
  preference it is and whether it is a non-negotiable, a constraint, or a
  trade-off.
- Identify which household responsibilities become harder during a solo-first
  period and what temporary support is actually available.

## 7. Define trial, return, and resilience gates

- Set a six-month trial plan: start date, review cadence, measures to inspect,
  and who participates in each review.
- Define a return or stop plan before moving: latest decision date, notice
  periods, ticket and housing exit costs, document/permit implications, work
  consequences, and the home-country landing plan.
- Calculate an emergency fund that covers the agreed household runway plus
  return costs and any time-limited gaps in income or health coverage.
- Identify adverse cases: delayed permit, job loss, second-income delay, rent
  increase, medical need, childcare failure, exchange-rate movement, or a
  required family visit. Model the material cases rather than averaging them
  away.
- Set explicit gates for professional advice. Obtain qualified immigration,
  tax, employment-law, financial, health, or education advice when the
  decision depends on a regulated interpretation, cross-border tax residence,
  dependent rights, contractual repayment, health coverage, or a material
  obligation that cannot be validated from official sources.

## 8. Final review before acting

- Confirm every material legal claim has an official source and current date.
- Confirm every material market input has a source, range, location scope, and
  as-of date.
- Confirm user facts have not been copied into public examples, documentation,
  logs, or research notes.
- Review each scenario's open questions, owner, evidence deadline, and effect
  if unanswered.
- Compare scenarios side by side as assumptions and trade-offs, including the
  stay-put and return paths. Do not collapse them into a composite score.
- Make the household decision separately, document the selected branch and its
  gates, and revisit it when a named trigger occurs.
