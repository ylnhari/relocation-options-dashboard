# Research methodology

Research records explain the assumptions behind an option; they do not calculate a winner. Each v5 `researchItem` records a topic, concise finding, applicable option IDs, status, publisher, source title, source URL, date, and note.

## Record evidence with its limits

Use `verified` only when the record supports the stated fact and remains applicable. Use `estimate` for an informed assumption or range. Use `question` when the information is missing, conditional, conflicting, or requires specialist confirmation. Preserve the date and publisher so a reader can judge freshness and authority.

Apply a record to all saved options by leaving its option-ID list empty, or list only the options to which it applies. Do not turn a general observation into a personal outcome. A research item can inform an input, but it does not replace evidence for that input.

## Source selection

Prefer primary, authoritative, and dateable sources for rules, official processes, and published charges. For market observations, use multiple current sources where practical and capture the assumptions behind the range. Record the exact claim being used rather than pasting extensive source material.

Before a high-impact decision, recheck time-sensitive topics such as taxes, eligibility, fees, housing availability, exchange rates, and employment terms. Seek qualified professional advice where appropriate.

## Reproducibility

Keep findings concise enough to review, link to the source when safe, and state any interpretation in the note. Never include unnecessary personal data, credentials, or copies of restricted documents. A valid schema record can still be stale or wrong; review evidence status, source, date, and uncertainty alongside every comparison.
